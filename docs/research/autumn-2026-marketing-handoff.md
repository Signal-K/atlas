# Marketing handoff: Autumn 2026 night-sky pilots

## Brief

Atlas is testing a small number of founder-led astronomy experiences while
the operational booking/CRM product is designed separately. The near-term
goal is not maximum reach. It is to find the first repeatable audience,
validate willingness to pay, and create memorable photos/stories that can
grow distribution.

Proposed pilots:

- Iceland, 6–10 October: premium “Iceland at Night” experience, one or two
  nights, with a local operator/astronomer.
- Tallinn/Lahemaa, 17 October: 8–12-person beginner night-sky experience.
- Tallinn/Estonia, 6 or 7 November: darker-sky/photo-focused capstone.

Dates are provisional until hosts, transport, weather policy and minimum
group economics are confirmed.

## Positioning

Lead with an honest promise: **small, human, science-led nights under real
skies**. The experience is the guide’s interpretation, the place, the
learning, the photographs and the group—not a guaranteed aurora or a generic
tour.

Possible campaign lines:

- “Learn the sky you are standing under.”
- “A small group. A darker place. A better way to meet the night sky.”
- “Aurora is possible; curiosity is guaranteed.”

Avoid “guaranteed Northern Lights”, generic bucket-list language, or implying
that Atlas is already a full booking marketplace.

## Audience and distribution

Start with warm and borrowed audiences because the owned social following is
small:

- existing Atlas users, waitlist and newsletter;
- astronomy clubs, university groups and science communicators;
- Tallinn/Tartu expat and photography communities;
- hotels, coworking spaces, tourism boards and local guides;
- Iceland/South Iceland accommodation and tour partners;
- founder and guest-partner personal networks.

Ask every partner for a concrete distribution action: email placement, social
post, community announcement, or a small allocation of seats. Partner trust
and direct invitations are more valuable for the first 8–12 seats than a broad
awareness campaign.

## Minimum asset kit per event

1. A single canonical event page with promise, date, price/deposit, group cap,
   meeting point, inclusions, weather policy and FAQ.
2. A reusable square/portrait poster generated from the event record, with
   variants for Instagram, stories, community groups and partner newsletters.
3. One 15–30 second vertical founder video: who it is for, what people will
   see/learn, and why the date/location was chosen.
4. Three short educational posts tied to the actual sky: Moon phase, Saturn,
   aurora science, or photography preparation.
5. A partner pack containing approved copy, the poster, canonical URL, dates,
   tracking code and a short safety/weather note.
6. Post-event: participant photo permissions, a small gallery, two attendee
   quotes, one behind-the-scenes story, and an Atlas journal/portfolio prompt.

## Funnel and experiments

Run a low-cost interest test first. The page should let people select city,
date window, audience type, photography interest and whether they would place a
deposit. Only confirm a date once the minimum viable group is visible.

Test one variable at a time:

- founder-led science evening vs. photo-first experience;
- Tallinn/Lahemaa vs. Tartu/observatory partner;
- direct invite vs. community partner vs. paid social;
- one-night Iceland experience vs. two-night package.

Track at minimum: source, landing-page conversion, waitlist-to-deposit,
deposit-to-attendance, cancellation/weather movement, acquisition cost by
channel, gross margin per attendee, referral rate, content permissions,
post-event satisfaction and Atlas official-tour portfolio creation.

## Handoff requests

Marketing should return:

- a recommended name and message hierarchy for the three pilots;
- two poster directions and a reusable event-information template;
- a partner outreach list for Tallinn, Tartu, South Iceland and one astronomy
  club/university per market;
- a four-week launch calendar for each confirmed event;
- a lightweight paid/organic test budget and success thresholds;
- the tracking convention for partner links and campaign codes;
- an explicit policy for weather changes, aurora claims and participant photo
  consent.

The events product owns event data, booking, CRM, partner operations, poster
production and social publishing. Atlas owns observing, Community and the
official-tour journal/portfolio/stamp experience; it may consume confirmed
tour metadata later through an integration.
